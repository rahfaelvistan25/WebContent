Part A

Multiple Choice

Select the best answer for each question. (15 Points — 1 pt each)

1. What does CMS stand for?
1 Point
Hint: Think about what the system manages: it's not physical computers, but the digital articles, media, and materials published on a website.
2. What is the primary purpose of a CMS?
1 Point
Hint: Consider why non-technical administrators use WordPress: it enables them to update announcements, articles, and pages without writing raw code.
3. Which component allows administrators to create and edit content?
1 Point
Hint: Look for the acronym starting with "Content Management..." that represents the administrative editing application.
4. What is the primary responsibility of a CDA?
1 Point
Hint: The "D" in CDA stands for "Delivery" — think about who consumes the content once published by the school admin.
5. Which of the following is an example of a CMS?
1 Point
Hint: One of these options is the open-source platform powering over 40% of the world's blogs and company websites.
6. What is the primary characteristic of a static website?
1 Point
Hint: In a static site, HTML files are pre-authored on disk and served as-is without on-demand database querying.
7. Which website type commonly retrieves information from a database?
1 Point
Hint: This type of website can assemble pages dynamically based on live user sessions, queries, and accounts.
8. What is a headless CMS?
1 Point
Hint: In web terminology, the "head" represents the presentation/theme layer. What is absent or omitted in a "headless" system?
9. Which technology is commonly used to connect a headless CMS to a frontend?
1 Point
Hint: An Application Programming Interface (like REST or GraphQL) used by mobile apps and frontend frameworks to fetch JSON data.
10. What is the purpose of a database in a CMS?
1 Point
Hint: Where are user accounts, article texts, timestamps, and permissions persistently saved and structured?
11. What does publishing mean in content management?
1 Point
Hint: Once an announcement shifts from "Draft" or "Pending Review" to live, what happens to its accessibility for website visitors?
12. Which CMS role generally has the highest level of control?
1 Point
Hint: This superuser role manages settings, plugins, themes, database updates, security, and all other user accounts.
13. What is content versioning?
1 Point
Hint: If an author makes an unintended change or error, this feature allows them to review the revision history and revert back.
14. What is one major advantage of a dynamic website?
1 Point
Hint: Think about logging into your student portal — why does it show YOUR grades and enrolled subjects rather than generic text?
15. Why are backups important in a CMS?
1 Point
Hint: If an administrator accidentally deletes the database or an attack occurs, what allows the school to recover its website data?
Part B

Identification

Identify the exact term, component, or concept described. (5 Points — 1 pt each)

1. The CMS component used by administrators to create and edit content.
1 Point
Hint: An abbreviation of 3 letters (or full 3-word phrase) beginning with "Content Management...".
2. The component responsible for delivering content to visitors.
1 Point
Hint: The delivery counterpart to CMA that processes visitor requests and merges database content with templates.
3. A website that primarily displays pre-created files.
1 Point
Hint: The opposite of a dynamic website — its files remain unchanged on the server until manually modified.
4. A CMS architecture that delivers content through APIs without requiring a traditional built-in frontend.
1 Point
Hint: Named using an anatomical metaphor referring to a body without a "head" (frontend theme).
5. The process of making approved content visible on a website.
1 Point
Hint: The decisive action that takes an article out of "Draft" or "Pending Review" and presents it live to visitors.
Part C

Short Answer Analysis

Answer each question concisely in 2–4 sentences. (10 Points — 2 pts each)

1. Explain the difference between static and dynamic websites.
2 Points

Suggested length: 2–4 complete sentences.

Hint: Compare how content is stored and served (pre-built files vs database-generated responses) and how updates are carried out.
2. Why is a CMS useful for schools and universities?
2 Points

Suggested length: 2–4 complete sentences.

Hint: Consider non-technical staff (admissions officers, teachers) who need to publish urgent deadlines without relying on web developers.
3. Explain how CMA, CDA, and a database work together.
2 Points

Suggested length: 2–4 complete sentences.

Hint: Trace the workflow chain: Who inputs the data (CMA)? Where is it safely kept (Database)? And who queries it and serves it to the browser (CDA)?
4. What is the importance of user roles and permissions?
2 Points

Suggested length: 2–4 complete sentences.

Hint: Think about security and governance: Should a student contributor have the authority to delete or modify the university homepage?
5. Give one real-world example of dynamic content management.
2 Points

Suggested length: 2–4 complete sentences.

Hint: Think of an online portal (school enrollment, online banking, an e-commerce shop, or news site) where each user sees different relevant information.
